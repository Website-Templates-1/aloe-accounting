## Why dividend planning matters
For many Canadian owner-managed businesses, dividends are a common way to pay shareholders. The tax result is not only about the amount you pay, it is also about the type of dividend (eligible or non-eligible) and whether the corporation makes the right designation.

While dividends are often called “tax-free money,” they are not tax-free. The CRA taxes shareholders, and the corporation must follow proper procedures and keep supporting documentation.

If you want help aligning dividend decisions with your year-end and tax situation, speak with our [tax planning](/services/tax-planning) team.

## When can a corporation pay dividends?
In general terms, a corporation can pay a dividend only when it has distributable retained earnings and its internal authorizations are in place.

Common practical steps include:
- **Confirm available retained earnings** (your accountant can help ensure the dividend is paid from appropriate corporate balances).
- **Have the dividend approved** in writing (for example, board resolutions).
- **Record the dividend properly** in your corporate books.
- **File required slips and track payments** so shareholder reporting matches what was paid.

Because dividend rules and corporate documentation requirements connect to your accounting records, keep your books organized. Our post on [audit-ready records](/blog/cra-bookkeeping-for-owner-managed-businesses-audit-ready-records) covers what CRA typically expects to see.

## Eligible vs non-eligible dividends, in plain language
Canada uses dividend categories because eligible dividends receive a more favourable federal tax treatment for many shareholders.

### Eligible dividends
Eligible dividends generally come from a corporation’s **eligible dividend designation** mechanism. When a corporation declares an eligible dividend, it also designates it for tax purposes so the shareholder can receive the enhanced tax credit.

### Non-eligible dividends
Non-eligible dividends (often associated with dividends that are not eligible) do not receive the same tax credit enhancement for shareholders.

In practice, the corporation’s ability to designate a dividend as eligible is connected to corporate tax accounts and the company’s history of earning taxes.

## The Eligible Dividend Designation (what it is and why it must be right)
The eligible dividend designation is the formal step that tells CRA and the shareholder what type of dividend was paid.

Key points:
- The **corporation designates** the dividend as eligible (or non-eligible).
- The designation has to be **supported by correct corporate records** and your tax profile for the period.
- Shareholders should receive tax slips that reflect the dividend type actually paid.

If the corporation designates incorrectly, you can end up with tax filing problems for shareholders and potential follow-up questions.

To reduce errors, many owner-managed businesses build dividend decisions into their annual tax workflow. If you are planning dividends around deadlines, review the [year-round tax season prep checklist](/blog/year-round-tax-season-prep-checklist-owner-managed-business-canada).

## What documentation should you keep?
The goal is to support both the corporate decision and the tax classification.

At a minimum, keep:
1. **Board resolutions** approving the dividend (and recording the dividend type, such as eligible or non-eligible).
2. **Dividend declaration and payment records** in your accounting system, showing the amounts and payment dates.
3. **Eligible dividend designation information** prepared for tax reporting. Your accountant will calculate the designation based on the corporation’s balances and tax accounts.
4. **Shareholder details** used for tax slips (so reporting is consistent with what was paid).
5. **T4 slips and T5 information**, if applicable, and any related CRA filing confirmations.
6. **Bank records** proving payments were made.

Also consider consolidating your supporting files so you can quickly respond to CRA questions. Clean bookkeeping is a lot easier than reconstructing documents later. If you would like a structure for this, see [audit-ready records](/blog/cra-bookkeeping-for-owner-managed-businesses-audit-ready-records).

## Common unwanted tax consequences to watch for
Here are the issues that typically create headaches:
- **Incorrect dividend classification** (eligible treated as non-eligible, or vice versa).
- **Missing or incomplete documentation**, making it hard to prove the dividend was properly authorized and designated.
- **Paying dividends when the corporation lacks sufficient retained earnings**, or without proper accounting support.
- **Mismatch between what the corporation paid and what was reported on tax slips**.

These problems can affect not only the corporation, but also shareholders who rely on the dividend slip to file their personal return.

## Practical next steps
Dividend planning is not just a year-end exercise. A practical approach is:
- Track year-to-date earnings and your expected available retained earnings.
- Decide dividend timing with your year-end and tax deadlines in mind.
- Prepare board resolutions and ensure the eligible dividend designation is correctly supported.
- Keep a tidy dividend file for each declaration and payment.

## FAQ

## What is the difference between eligible and non-eligible dividends in Canada?
Eligible dividends generally come with a more favourable tax credit for eligible shareholders, while non-eligible dividends do not receive the same enhanced credit.

Can a corporation pay both eligible and non-eligible dividends at the same time?
Yes, it is possible depending on your corporate tax account balances and how the dividends are designated. The key is that each dividend type must be supported and properly designated.

Do shareholders choose whether dividends are eligible?
No. The corporation designates the dividend type based on its tax accounts and documentation. Shareholders then report based on the tax slips they receive.

What happens if the eligible dividend designation is wrong?
Incorrect designations can cause reporting issues for shareholders and may lead to CRA questions. Correct paperwork and accountant support help reduce this risk.

What records should I keep for dividend payments?
Keep board resolutions, accounting entries, dividend declarations, eligible designation support, proof of payment (bank records), and tax slips prepared from the amounts paid.

Can I pay a dividend anytime during the year?
Dividends should be declared only when the corporation has sufficient distributable retained earnings and after proper approvals are in place. Timing often aligns with year-end tax preparation and planning.
