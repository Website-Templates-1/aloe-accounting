## Why mixed-use documentation matters
Owner-managers often use the same vehicle, phone, or computer for both work and personal life. The CRA generally allows business expenses only to the extent they relate to earning business income. For mixed-use assets, that means you need a reasonable method to calculate the business-use portion, and records that support it.

If you cannot show how you arrived at the claim, the CRA may reduce or disallow expenses. In some cases, it can also affect your GST/HST treatment, especially if you claim input tax credits.

For audit-ready housekeeping, see [audit-ready bookkeeping records](/blog/cra-bookkeeping-for-owner-managed-businesses-audit-ready-records).

## What the CRA typically expects
While every situation is different, CRA expectations for mixed-use property usually come down to three themes:

1. **Clear business purpose**: The expense should relate to earning business income (not personal convenience).
2. **Reasonable allocation method**: You need a consistent way to split costs between business and personal use.
3. **Support you can produce**: Your numbers should tie to source documents (invoices, logs, calendars, device details, and mileage records).

## Vehicles: track mileage, not just the total cost
For a vehicle used for both personal and business travel, the business-use portion is typically based on mileage or another usage measure. The most common approach is a log that records:

- Date of trip
- Starting location and destination
- Business purpose (brief note)
- Kilometres driven

**What to keep**
- Mileage log with enough detail to understand the business purpose
- Receipts for fuel, maintenance, repairs, parking, and tolls (where available)
- Insurance and registration documents
- Records showing when the vehicle was available for business use (for example, purchase date)

**Common audit triggers for vehicles**
- No log or only partial log coverage (for example, claiming a large business percentage without supporting days)
- Claiming “work trips” that cannot be explained or do not appear tied to clients or business activity
- Using a changing method year to year without documentation

If you use your vehicle across roles and entities, it is also important to align the tax treatment. Consider whether you are claiming in a corporation, on a T1 personal return, or across both. ## Phones and internet: keep proof and document the split
For mobile devices and home internet used for both personal and business, you generally need to estimate the business portion and back it up with reasonable support.

**What to keep**
- Monthly phone or internet invoices
- Device information (provider statements, plan details, and term dates)
- A written method for your estimate (for example, number of business calls, hours used for work apps, or an allocation based on usage)
- Notes for major changes (switching plans, getting a second line, new work patterns)

**Practical tip**
If you estimate based on usage, use the same basis consistently. CRA reviewers often look for an allocation method that is logical, repeatable, and not “tuned” to match deductions.

**Common audit triggers for phones and internet**
- Claiming 100% business use when you also use the same line for personal activities
- Estimates with no explanation or no support documents
- Large swings year over year with no recorded reason

## Computers and office equipment: separate personal ownership from business use
Computers, laptops, and tablets used for mixed purposes can create problems if documentation is weak. If you purchased the asset personally but used it for business, you need to be able to show the business-use portion and how it was allocated.

**What to keep**
- Purchase invoice, date, and cost
- Evidence the device was used for business (for example, business emails, software licenses tied to work, or files created for business)
- Your allocation method (for example, estimated business-use percentage based on time or primary user activity)
- Notes about when business use started (and when it changed)

**Common audit triggers for computers**
- No purchase records, or claiming deductions without any invoice or purchase date
- Very high business-use estimates without support
- Inconsistent reporting across years (for example, 80% one year and 20% the next without explanation)

## GST/HST considerations for mixed-use
Mixed-use can affect GST/HST input tax credits. If you claim input tax credits, be prepared to support:

- Whether the purchase was for commercial activities
- Your allocation method for the business portion
- The timing and tax amounts on the supplier invoices

## How to avoid common CRA red flags
Here are practical steps that reduce risk:

- **Use a written allocation method** and apply it consistently across the year.
- **Keep supporting source documents** (invoices, receipts, logs, statements).
- **Document key changes** (new phone plan, vehicle swap, schedule change, added business duties).
- **Avoid round-number claims** that look arbitrary. If you claim 75%, explain how you arrived at it.
- **Don’t mix personal and business documents loosely**. Store everything for the asset in one place.
