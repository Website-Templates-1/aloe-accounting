## Why year-end tax planning matters (for incorporated owner-managers)
If you are an owner-manager of a Canadian corporation, your tax outcomes can depend on decisions made before your fiscal year-end. While nothing should be rushed or assumed, a plan can help you avoid last-minute surprises with corporate taxes, personal taxes, and GST/HST timing.

This guide covers evergreen moves you can consider before December 31. For anything that depends on your situation, timelines, or elections, speak with your advisor.

If you want a structured approach, our team supports owner-managed businesses with [tax planning](/services/tax-planning).

## 1) Review compensation planning: salary vs dividends
One of the biggest year-end questions is how to pay yourself: salary, bonuses, or dividends.

Common planning considerations include:
- **Salary and bonuses**: Generally create deductible corporate expenses, and they may come with payroll source deductions and filing requirements.
- **Dividends**: Not deductible to the corporation, but may be more tax efficient in some cases depending on your overall income.
- **Cash flow vs tax timing**: You may be able to adjust planned amounts based on what the corporation expects to earn and what you can actually pay.

The key is to confirm your corporation has the capacity to pay, and that any dividend decisions follow CRA requirements and proper documentation. Our [personal tax](/services/personal-tax) and [corporate tax](/services/corporate-tax) groups can help you think through the tradeoffs.

## 2) Confirm your year-end bookkeeping is audit-ready
Closing the books is not just an accounting exercise, it is also the foundation for tax calculations. A missed invoice, an incorrect accrual, or an incomplete supporting document can lead to backtracking.

Consider this practical step:
- Ensure receipts, contracts, and summaries are filed and tied to the correct accounts.
- Reconcile bank and credit card accounts.
- Review payroll records, including year-end totals.

If you are building stronger support for CRA inquiries, see [audit-ready records](/blog/cra-bookkeeping-for-owner-managed-businesses-audit-ready-records).

## 3) Review year-end accruals and prepaid expenses
Many expenses are deductible when properly incurred and supported, but timing matters. Before year-end, review items such as:
- **Accrued expenses**: Professional fees, rent, utilities, and other services received but not yet invoiced.
- **Bonuses or wages**: Only accrue amounts that meet the corporation’s entitlement requirements and are supported by documentation.
- **Prepaid expenses**: Some costs may need to be handled differently for accounting versus tax, depending on the nature of the expense.

A common approach is to create a short accrual list, confirm whether the work was completed by year-end, and ensure the amounts are reasonable and supported.

## 4) Do closing entries, then recheck the tax impact
Closing entries translate your trial balance into accurate financial statements and tax forms. Before finalizing, recheck:
- Related-party transactions (if any) and reasonable documentation.
- Fixed asset additions and disposals (and ensure tax treatment aligns with accounting records).
- Any deferred items you previously recorded.

If you need help coordinating the accounting close with tax reporting, consider our [business advisory](/services/business-advisory) services.

## 5) Spousal and family considerations, where appropriate
For incorporated owner-managers, spousal considerations can come up in two main ways:
- **Compensation to a spouse** (salary or fees), which must be supported by genuine work and proper documentation.
- **Estate and planning context**, which is not only tax technical, but also depends on your family goals.

Important note: CRA scrutiny can increase when payments to spouses or family members are made without clear business reasons and supporting records. If you are considering a spouse-related plan, review it early rather than at year-end.

## 6) Don’t ignore GST/HST timing and compliance
While this post focuses on income tax planning, GST/HST can affect your year-end numbers and cash flow. Before you finalize returns:
- Confirm GST/HST collected and paid is recorded in the correct period.
- Review any input tax credits you plan to claim.
- Reconcile GST/HST accounts to your general ledger.

For year-round readiness, you may also find our [tax season prep checklist](/blog/year-round-tax-season-prep-checklist-owner-managed-business-canada) useful.

## 7) Check installment and cash planning items
Depending on your corporation’s taxes payable, installment requirements can apply. Make sure you know whether you have obligations and deadlines so you are not forced into last-minute cash decisions.

If you are unsure, review [corporate tax instalments](/blog/corporate-tax-instalments-when-required-how-to-avoid-penalties) to confirm what applies to your situation.

## A practical before Dec 31 checklist
Here is a short checklist to start now:
- Confirm year-end compensation approach (salary, bonus, dividends) and document decisions.
- Identify accrued expenses and prepaid items, then support with records.
- Complete reconciliations and verify bookkeeping accuracy.
- Prepare closing entries and recheck key accounts (assets, payroll, related-party items).
- Review GST/HST balances and input tax credits.
- Confirm installment and filing deadlines for your corporation.
