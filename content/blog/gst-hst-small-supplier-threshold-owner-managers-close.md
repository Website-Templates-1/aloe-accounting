## The GST/HST small supplier threshold, in plain language
In Canada, many owner-managed businesses do not have to register for GST/HST right away if they meet the “small supplier” threshold. The general threshold is **$30,000** of taxable supplies in a single calendar quarter and in the last four consecutive calendar quarters.

If you are under the threshold, you generally do not charge GST/HST to customers. If you exceed it, you may have to register and start charging GST/HST sooner than expected.

## What counts toward the $30,000
The $30,000 measure is based on **taxable supplies** (not total revenue).

In practice, it usually includes sales where GST/HST would apply, such as:
- taxable goods and services you sell in Canada
- taxable portions of mixed transactions (where applicable)

It can exclude certain amounts that are not taxable, such as some exempt supplies and many zero-rated supplies. Because the details depend on what you do, it is worth confirming how your revenue should be treated.

## How the “lookback” period works
CRA generally looks at:
- the **current calendar quarter**, and
- the **last four consecutive calendar quarters**

This is why businesses near the line need to monitor sales regularly. If your taxable supplies creep above $30,000, registration can become required.

## When the threshold is prorated
If you start operating partway through a year, CRA may require the threshold to be **prorated** for the period you were active.

Common situations include:
- starting a new business during the year (for example, you incorporate or begin operations partway through a calendar year)
- certain changes in ownership or structure that affect how CRA views the business as “the same” supplier

Proration is not automatic for every scenario. The key point is that you should not assume $30,000 is always available in full when you are only operating for part of the year.

For owner-managers, this is also where good bookkeeping matters, because taxable supplies need to be tracked by quarter. See [audit-ready records](/blog/cra-bookkeeping-for-owner-managed-businesses-audit-ready-records) for practical steps to keep your numbers consistent.

## What triggers GST/HST registration
Registration can be triggered if you:
1. **Exceed the $30,000 threshold** based on the CRA “lookback” test, or
2. Are required to register for other reasons even if you are below $30,000 (for example, where specific activities make registration mandatory).

In many cases, the most important planning trigger is exceeding the threshold in the quarters CRA is reviewing.

### Practical planning tip: watch quarterly taxable supplies
If you are approaching the limit, you can reduce surprises by:
- reviewing taxable supplies each month (or at least each quarter)
- separating taxable, exempt, and zero-rated sales in your records
- flagging one-time or project-based contracts that could push you over

## Once you register, what you may have to do
If you become required to register, you generally have to start:
- charging GST/HST on taxable sales
- filing GST/HST returns on your required schedule

Whether you have to deal with prior period adjustments, including tax collected versus tax owing, depends on the facts. This is one reason many businesses want a timely review when they are close to the threshold.

## Owner-manager considerations that commonly get overlooked
- **New entity, new activity, or major change:** A switch to incorporation, a change in partners, or a restructure can affect how CRA treats the supplier.
- **Mixed transactions:** If you sell both taxable and exempt or zero-rated items, the taxable portion matters.
- **Contract timing:** If revenue is recognized differently for GST/HST purposes than for income tax, you can misjudge where you stand. Keeping GST/HST-focused tracking helps.
