As your business grows, payroll compliance can become harder to manage. Workflows change, staff turnover happens, and it’s easier to miss a due date or enter information incorrectly.

Below are common watch-outs for owner-managed Canadian businesses, focused on three areas that commonly trigger CRA follow-ups: ROEs (Record of Employment), remittances, and T4/T5 slip reporting.

## 1) ROEs: avoid timing and “missing information” errors
An ROE is required when an employee experiences an interruption of earnings and is still employed by the employer under specific conditions (and may also be required in other situations depending on the circumstances). Many problems come from treating the ROE like a “nice-to-have” administrative step.

Common missteps include:
- **Delaying the ROE** until the employee asks or until after an end-of-month payroll run.
- **Submitting an ROE with incorrect dates** (start/end of the interruption, or earnings-related dates).
- **Not matching payroll history** to what appears on the ROE. If the ROE period doesn’t align with actual earnings, you may get questions later.
- **Inconsistent employer information** (business number, payroll account, or contact details).
- **Using the wrong ROE reason code** (or applying a reason based on assumptions rather than the interruption facts).

Practical prevention tips:
- **Create an ROE trigger** in your payroll process (for example: when you change an employee’s status to “no pay” or an interruption begins).
- **Check the pay history** before you submit: confirm the earnings pattern matches what you plan to report.
- **Keep a simple ROE log** (employee, interruption start date, submission date, file/reference number).

## 2) Remittances: timing errors are usually the costly ones
Payroll remittances typically include **source deductions** (CPP, EI, and income tax withheld). CRA expects you to remit on time based on your remittance frequency and filing schedule.

Common missteps include:
- **Late remittances** due to manual payment timing or missed bank transfer deadlines.
- **Paying “what you think you owe”** instead of what your payroll reports calculate.
- **Not updating remittance frequency** if your business’s activity changes.
- **Reclassification issues** (for example, incorrect treatment of taxable benefits or taxable vs. non-taxable items) that can affect withholding.
- **Remitting based on the wrong payroll run** (timing differences between when the employee is paid and when amounts are considered remittable).

Practical prevention tips:
- **Reconcile after each payroll**: source deductions withheld per payroll report should tie to what you plan to remit.
- **Use calendar-based reminders** tied to your CRA remittance dates.
- **Set a “remittance ownership” rule**: one person must be responsible for verifying totals and payment scheduling.

## 3) T4 and T5: avoid slip errors and year-end reconciliation gaps
T4 slips report employment income and related deductions. T5 slips report certain types of investment income (for example, interest paid by the payer).

Even when payroll runs correctly throughout the year, issues show up at year-end when slips are prepared.

Common missteps include:
- **Incorrect social insurance number (SIN)** or missing employee identifiers.
- **Miscalculated boxes** (CPP/EI exemptions, taxable benefits, or other payroll classifications).
- **Not reconciling payroll summaries** to the intended slip totals.
- **Filing slips without reviewing year-end totals** against your payroll ledger.
- **T5 confusion**: businesses sometimes forget that certain payments may require T5 reporting, or they fail to track payer/payee details accurately throughout the year.

Practical prevention tips:
- **Run a year-end reconciliation early** (before final pay adjustments). Compare totals from payroll reports to what will populate T4 boxes.
- **Verify employee data** (name and SIN) throughout the year, not just at December.
- **Track T5-relevant payments** in your accounting system during the year so you aren’t reconstructing details at year-end.

## 4) The “penalty risk” often comes from process gaps, not effort
Many compliance problems are avoidable with consistent checks:
- Timelines (ROE trigger and submission timing, remittance calendar, slip review deadlines)
- Accuracy (dates, reason codes, deduction totals, employee identifiers)
- Reconciliation (payroll reports to remittance totals and slip totals)

If you’ve had CRA requests for adjustments, or your accounting and payroll systems don’t reconcile cleanly, it can help to have your workflow reviewed.

For growing businesses, payroll compliance is less about “knowing every rule” and more about building reliable timing and reconciliation habits.
