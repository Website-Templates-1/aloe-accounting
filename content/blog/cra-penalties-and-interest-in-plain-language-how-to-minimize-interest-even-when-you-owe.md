## CRA penalties vs interest, in plain language
If you owe the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), you may face two different types of charges:

- **Penalties** for certain failures, like filing late, missing instalments, or not remitting required amounts.
- **Interest** on unpaid balances, which typically accrues from the due date until the amount is paid.

Even if you cannot pay everything immediately, actions you take early can sometimes reduce how much interest and penalty you end up owing.

## Common sources of CRA penalties and interest for owner-managed businesses
Below are frequent triggers we see for penalties and related interest.

### 1) Late or incomplete filings
Examples include late **corporate income tax returns**, late **personal returns**, or late **GST/HST returns**.

Why this matters: CRA may charge a **late-filing penalty**, and any taxes still owing can also attract **interest**.

A year-round approach helps. See our [tax season prep checklist](/blog/year-round-tax-season-prep-checklist-owner-managed-business-canada) for practical planning ideas.

### 2) Missed or incorrect instalments (corporate or personal)
If instalments are required and you pay late or underpay, CRA can assess penalties. Interest may also apply to balances that remain unpaid.

If you have a corporation, it is worth reviewing whether instalments are required for your situation and how instalment payments are calculated. Our guide on [tax instalments](/blog/corporate-tax-instalments-when-required-how-to-avoid-penalties) can help you spot common problem areas.

### 3) GST/HST remittances and filing timing
Typical issues include:

- remitting GST/HST late
- missing the due date for a GST/HST return
- using the wrong reporting period or business accounting method

When GST/HST is not remitted on time, interest can accrue on the unpaid amount.

### 4) Payroll remittances (source deductions)
For payroll, CRA expects you to deduct and remit on time. Common issues include:

- remitting **withholding tax, CPP, and EI** late
- failing to file related payroll remittances paperwork on time
- incorrect payroll calculations that lead to short remittances

Payroll issues can be particularly costly because CRA charges apply to the amounts that should have been remitted.

### 5) Trust accounts and “trust” remittances
taxes such as GST/HST collected from customers and payroll deductions are often treated as **amounts held in trust** for CRA. Even if the company plans to pay later, CRA expects these amounts to be remitted when due.

## Practical steps to reduce interest, even if you owe
Interest is usually calculated on the unpaid portion. That means your best opportunities are typically about (1) paying what you can earlier and (2) avoiding further avoidable failures.

### Step 1: Pay something as soon as you can
If you cannot pay the full balance, paying a **partial amount** can reduce the portion that continues to accrue interest. Discuss the best approach for your balance and due dates with your accountant.

### Step 2: File first, even if payment is not ready
If you are behind on filing, try to file as soon as possible. Filing later can lead to additional penalties on top of ongoing interest.

### Step 3: Correct errors quickly
If you realize there is a mistake, act fast. For example:

- GST/HST returns filed with incorrect amounts
- payroll remittances that were under-calculated
- corporate return information that needs correction

A quicker correction can help limit compounding issues.

### Step 4: Use organized bookkeeping to support CRA positions
CRA often expects good records. When you are trying to resolve balances or respond to questions, having clean documentation reduces delays.

You can start with audit-ready record habits. Our [CRA bookkeeping for owner-managed businesses](/blog/cra-bookkeeping-for-owner-managed-businesses-audit-ready-records) post includes practical steps.

### Step 5: Review instalment planning before you fall further behind
If instalments are part of your tax profile, adjust planning so you do not repeat the cycle. Our [corporate tax planning](/services/tax-planning) services can help you set a realistic process for forecasting and payments.

### Step 6: Ask about CRA options when you are unable to pay in full
Depending on the facts, CRA may have payment arrangements. Getting advice before you contact CRA can prevent missteps and help you prepare what CRA will ask for.

Our [business advisory](/services/business-advisory) and [CRA representation](/services/cra-representation) teams can help you organize your documentation and communicate clearly.

## When you should act immediately
Consider urgent action if you have any of the following:

- repeated late filings or missed payroll remittances
- a growing balance due plus missed instalments
- notices or reassessments that require a response

Delays usually increase interest. The sooner you file, pay what you can, and clean up the underlying issues, the better your position.

## Bottom line
CRA penalties and interest can be stressful, but there are practical steps you can take to minimize the damage, especially if you act early. Focus on filing on time, paying at least a partial amount when possible, correcting errors quickly, and building recordkeeping that supports your position.
