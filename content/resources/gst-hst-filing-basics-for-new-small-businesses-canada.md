Starting a new business in Canada can be exciting—and complicated fast. If you sell goods or services, GST/HST may apply. Below is a plain-language guide to the key GST/HST filing basics for small businesses, including registration thresholds, filing frequency, and common mistakes.

*Note: GST/HST rules can vary depending on your situation. For help applying this to your business, contact ALOE Accounting and Tax.*

## 1) Do you need to register for GST/HST?
In many cases, you must register if your taxable supplies in Canada exceed the **GST/HST registration threshold**.

- **General rule:** If your total taxable revenues are **over the threshold** over a **12-month period** (or expected to be), you generally need to register.
- **“Taxable supplies” matters:** Not every dollar you receive counts the same way. Sales that are GST/HST-taxable typically count toward the threshold, while certain exempt supplies may not.
- **If you exceed the threshold mid-year:** You’ll generally need to register and start charging GST/HST from the appropriate time.

If you’re unsure whether your sales count toward the threshold (or whether your products/services are taxable, exempt, or zero-rated), it’s worth confirming before filing.

## 2) When you register, what changes?
Once registered:

- You generally have to **charge GST/HST** on taxable sales.
- You can generally **claim input tax credits (ITCs)** for the GST/HST you paid on business expenses (as long as the expenses relate to your commercial activities and are eligible).
- You must **file GST/HST returns** and remit any net tax (the difference between collected GST/HST and claimable ITCs).

## 3) Filing frequency: how often do you file?
Your GST/HST filing frequency is usually based on your reporting situation and the CRA program setup. Common frequencies include:

- **Annual** filing (with periodic remittances in some cases)
- **Quarterly** filing
- **Monthly** filing

What determines your frequency?

- Your expected or actual GST/HST reporting amounts
- Whether you elect a different reporting period (in some cases)
- CRA’s assignment based on your registration details

After you register, check your CRA account or your registration correspondence for the filing schedule that applies to you. Missing the correct due dates is one of the easiest ways to run into penalties.

## 4) What goes on a GST/HST return?
A typical GST/HST return includes:

- **GST/HST collected** on your sales for the reporting period
- **GST/HST paid** on eligible business expenses (ITCs)
- **Adjustments** if needed (for example, certain corrections or changes)
- **Net amount** due (or refund, if you have more ITCs than collected tax)

Keep supporting records so you can back up the numbers if CRA asks questions.

## 5) Common GST/HST mistakes new small businesses make
Here are practical issues we see often when businesses are getting started:

### a) Charging GST/HST incorrectly
- Charging GST/HST when you shouldn’t (e.g., mixing up exempt vs taxable items)
- Not charging GST/HST on taxable sales
- Using the wrong rate for the customer’s province

### b) Claiming ITCs without eligible support
ITCs are generally only available for eligible purchases. Common problems include:
- Missing invoices or receipts that show the GST/HST separately
- Claiming ITCs on expenses that are not related to your commercial activities
- Treating personal expenses as business expenses

### c) Missing filing deadlines
If you file late or miss remittances, CRA may charge penalties and interest. Set reminders for:
- Return due dates
- Payment due dates (if different)

### d) Forgetting about credit notes and adjustments
If you issue refunds, credit notes, or allowances later, the GST/HST impact must be handled correctly.

### e) Mismatching bookkeeping and GST/HST records
A common operational issue is having clean income statements but messy GST/HST tracking. Ideally, your accounting system should:
- Track GST/HST amounts on sales and expenses
- Support your return totals

## 6) Keep good records from day one
Good recordkeeping makes filing easier and reduces errors. Consider keeping:

- Sales records (invoices, receipts, POS reports)
- Purchase records (supplier invoices showing GST/HST)
- A clear method for tracking GST/HST by rate (where applicable)
- Notes on any exempt/zero-rated treatment you’re using

## 7) Getting GST/HST right early is worth it
GST/HST compliance doesn’t have to be complicated, but it does need consistency. If you’re newly registered—or still deciding whether you should register—getting the basics right early can prevent rework and avoid costly filing issues.
