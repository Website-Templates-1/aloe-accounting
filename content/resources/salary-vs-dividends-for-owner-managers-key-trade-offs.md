## Why this choice matters
If you own and control a Canadian corporation, you generally have flexibility in how you extract money from the company. Two common options are:

- **Salary (employment income)**
- **Dividends (distributions from corporate profits)**

Both can work, but they come with different tax and compliance implications. The “best” approach depends on your total income situation, your corporation’s earnings, and your broader retirement and cash-flow goals. This guide stays general; consider talking with ALOE Accounting and Tax about your circumstances.

## Salary: what you get (and what it costs)
**Salary** is paid under an employment relationship and is generally **deductible to the corporation** (if properly structured and supported). You report the salary on your personal return as employment income.

Key trade-offs:

- **CPP and EI may apply (depending on facts):**
  - In many owner-manager situations, salary can trigger **CPP contributions** (and sometimes EI depending on eligibility).
  - CPP is not “free,” but it can increase your retirement benefits.
- **Withholding and payroll compliance:**
  - Salary requires payroll setup and regular payroll remittances (e.g., source deductions).
- **RRSP room can be impacted:**
  - Employment income can increase your **RRSP contribution room**.
  - RRSP room for most people is tied to earned income, subject to CRA rules.
- **No “dividend tax credit” mechanism:**
  - Tax treatment differs from dividends. Your marginal rate and overall income determine the final outcome.

## Dividends: common benefits and considerations
**Dividends** come from corporate after-tax earnings and are paid to shareholders. Dividends are **not payroll income**, so the corporation generally doesn’t deduct dividends the way it deducts salary.

Key trade-offs:

- **No CPP on dividends:**
  - Dividends generally do **not** create CPP contributions because they are not employment earnings.
  - This can matter if you want to build or protect CPP retirement benefits.
- **Dividend tax rates and the eligible vs non-eligible split:**
  - Canadian dividend taxation depends on whether dividends are **eligible** or **non-eligible**.
- **Corporate and legal requirements:**
  - Dividends generally require appropriate corporate approvals and are paid only from available earnings.
- **RRSP room: may be limited:**
  - Since dividends are not employment income, they generally **don’t increase RRSP room** the same way salary does (though other earned income sources could still provide room).

## The balancing factors owner-managers often weigh
### 1) Your personal income and tax bracket
Higher personal income can increase the marginal tax rate. Because salary and dividends have different tax mechanics, the “break-even” point varies for each person.

### 2) CPP planning
If your priority is maximizing **CPP entitlement**, salary may help because it can generate CPP contributions. If cash-flow efficiency is the focus and you already have other earned income, dividends may be more attractive. The right answer depends on your broader situation.

### 3) RRSP and retirement contributions
If you’re trying to increase **RRSP contribution room** and build retirement savings, salary may be useful because it typically counts as earned income for RRSP purposes. Dividends typically don’t create the same RRSP room.

### 4) Cash flow and payroll administration
- Salary means ongoing payroll responsibilities (withholding, remittances, T4 reporting).
- Dividends can be simpler administratively, but require corporate compliance (e.g., dividend declarations) and careful tax planning around retained earnings.

### 5) The corporation’s tax position and available earnings
Your corporation’s situation—such as how much profit is retained, and the nature of its income—can affect what kind of dividends are feasible and when.

## A practical way to approach the decision
Many owner-managers use a planning approach such as:

1. **Start with your personal target** (e.g., the income you need in the year, plus retirement savings goals).
2. **Map impacts**: CPP, RRSP room, personal tax, and corporate payroll responsibilities.
3. **Consider time and flexibility**: Your optimal mix may change as your income, age, and corporation earnings change.
4. **Keep documentation and compliance tight**: Whether you pay salary or dividends, the paperwork matters.

## What to discuss with your advisor
When you meet with ALOE Accounting and Tax, it helps to bring:

## Bottom line
Salary and dividends each have strengths and limitations. Salary can support CPP contributions and RRSP room, while dividends avoid payroll deductions but come with different tax treatment and corporate requirements. A balanced plan often depends on your personal bracket, retirement planning, and how much flexibility your corporation has.
