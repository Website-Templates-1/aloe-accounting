Running a business means tracking expenses. But not everything you spend is automatically deductible. The Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) generally looks at whether an expense is incurred to earn business income and whether it’s reasonable, supported by records, and properly allocated when it’s partly personal.

Below is a practical guide to some common categories—especially meals, home office, and vehicles—plus what documentation you should keep.

## The basics: when is an expense deductible?
In general, an expense may be deductible if it:

- Relates to earning income from your business (a clear business purpose)
- Is reasonable in amount
- Is supported by adequate records
- Is not “personal or living expenses” (unless there’s a specific rule that allows allocation)

Even if an expense seems “business-related,” CRA can disallow it if the connection to income is weak or the records aren’t there.

If you’re unsure whether an item qualifies, it’s worth speaking with your accountant.

## Meals: business meals can be deductible (with limits)
Meals are often deductible when they’re incurred to earn business income—typical examples include client meetings or meals during travel for business purposes.

Key points to remember:

- **Keep receipts** that show the date, location, who attended (when relevant), and the amount.
- **Document the business purpose.** A simple note such as “client meeting re: quarterly plan” can be helpful.
- **Know the “reasonable” expectation.** CRA may challenge meals that appear excessive.

Also, meal deductions are subject to **specific limits and tax rules**. If you’re claiming meal expenses regularly, confirm the correct treatment for your situation with ALOE Accounting and Tax.

## Home office: only the business-use portion is deductible
If you work from home, you may be able to deduct certain home office expenses, but CRA expects that the space is used **regularly and for the purpose of earning business income**.

Common home office expenses can include:

- A portion of rent (if you rent)
- A portion of utilities (e.g., hydro, heat, internet)
- Home insurance (business-use portion)
- Repairs and maintenance (business-use portion)
- Office supplies
- Sometimes depreciation, depending on your circumstances and how you own the property

How to support it:

- **Measure the workspace** (e.g., square footage) and keep your calculation.
- **Track your method** (commonly a square-footage or similar reasonable allocation). Use the same method consistently.
- **Keep bills/receipts** for utilities and other costs.
- Be ready to show that the area is used for business—not just occasionally for paperwork.

If you have mixed personal and business use, you generally must claim only the portion that relates to business activity.

## Vehicle expenses: deductible only for business use
Vehicle expenses are one of the most scrutinized categories. If you use a vehicle for both personal and business purposes, you must track the **business-use portion**.

You may be able to deduct:

- Gas, repairs, maintenance, insurance (portion related to business use)
- Parking and tolls
- Certain vehicle-related costs, depending on the type of vehicle and your situation

To claim vehicle expenses properly:

- **Keep a log** of business kilometres versus total kilometres.
- Record details like **date, destination, and business purpose**.
- Keep **receipts** for expenses you’re claiming.
- Separate personal and business trips—don’t estimate without records.

CRA expects your records to be credible. A spreadsheet or app can work, but it still needs to be complete enough to support your claims.

## Other common deductible expense categories (and what to watch)
While meals, home office, and vehicles are frequent areas of questions, other categories that often come up include:

- **Office supplies and equipment** used for the business
- **Professional services** (accounting, legal, bookkeeping)
- **Bank fees and interest** related to operating the business
- **Advertising and marketing**
- **Licences and subscriptions**
- **Insurance premiums** related to business risk
- **Travel** related to earning business income

What to watch for:

- Items that can blur into personal spending (clothing, phone/internet, certain meals)
- Big purchases without supporting documentation
- Expenses claimed without explaining the business purpose

## Documentation: what CRA typically expects
Good record-keeping is what turns “maybe deductible” into “defensible.” Aim to keep:

- Receipts/invoices for each expense claimed
- Proof of payment when available
- Notes on business purpose for meals and travel
- Calculations for allocations (home office, partly personal vehicles)
- Mileage logs for vehicles

If you use accounting software, keep your source documents organized and tied to the transactions.

## When in doubt, get clarity before you file
Deductibility can depend on details—like whether a space is genuinely used for business, the nature of travel, or how a vehicle is used throughout the year.

### Want a simple checklist?
- Are the expenses connected to earning business income?
- Can you show the business purpose (especially meals and travel)?
- Do you have receipts/invoices?
- For home office and vehicle: did you track and calculate only the business-use portion?
- Are your records complete and consistent for the year (including your fiscal year-end)?

Keeping it organized now makes tax filing smoother later—even if you’re busy running the business today.
