## Why year-round prep matters
Tax season feels overwhelming partly because the records you need are scattered across accounts, emails, and spreadsheets. For owner-managed businesses, a simple routine—monthly and quarterly—can make year-end faster, cleaner, and less stressful.

Below is a practical checklist you can use throughout the year. It’s general guidance for Canadian owner-managed businesses. If you’d like help building a schedule that fits your business, contact ALOE Accounting and Tax.

---

## Month-by-month checklist (keep this ongoing)
### 1) Bank and credit records
Gather and reconcile these monthly:
- **Bank statements** (all business accounts)
- **Monthly bank reconciliations** and any outstanding items list
- **Credit card statements** and merchant invoices (business card)
- **Electronic payments and deposits** (e-transfer, online banking exports)
- **Inter-account transfers** (so they don’t look like taxable or business income)

Tip: Reconcile monthly, not quarterly. It’s much easier to spot errors early.

### 2) Sales and revenue support
For businesses that issue invoices/receipts:
- **Invoice register** (or accounting system export)
- **Sales receipts** (including cash sales if you have them)
- **Debit/credit notes** and refund documentation
- **GST/HST collection summary** (by rate, if applicable)

If you use accounting software, keep it set up so you can pull a monthly report by tax rate.

### 3) Expenses and receipts
Collect and categorize expenses monthly:
- **Supplier invoices** and supporting receipts
- **Monthly expense summaries** from your accounting system
- **Meals, travel, and entertainment** receipts (with business purpose where required)
- **Vehicle logs or mileage tracking** (if you claim vehicle expenses)
- **Home office records** (if you claim—square footage, utilities, and method)

Make a rule: no receipt, no category. If you can’t locate the receipt later, it’s harder to support the claim.

### 4) Payroll documentation (if you have employees)
Each payroll period, keep:
- **Pay stubs** (employee copies)
- **Payroll register / payroll summary reports**
- **Employer remittance confirmation** for CPP/EI and payroll deductions
- **T4/TPD setup info** (by year, if you’re on track early)

If you run payroll through a service provider, download the monthly reports and store them.

---

## Quarterly checklist (tighten up before year-end)
### 5) Contractor and subcontractor records
At least quarterly, confirm you have:
- **Contractor invoices**
- **Proof of payment** (bank/cheque confirmation)
- **Any tax documentation you received** (for example, provincial forms, if applicable)
- **Contact details** (name, address, business number where available)

By the end of the year, you’ll want to be able to quickly identify who was paid and how much.

### 6) GST/HST and remittance support
For GST/HST filings, keep quarterly support for:
- **Input tax credits (ITCs)** totals by period
- **Sales tax collected** by rate
- **Reconciliations** between your books and GST/HST returns
- **CRA correspondence** related to GST/HST accounts
- **Any adjustments** (credit notes, rebates, corrections)

If you use multiple locations or tax rates, keep the breakdown clean.

### 7) CRA account tracking (don’t wait until April)
Create a folder (digital or physical) for CRA communications and statements:
- **CRA My Business Account notices**
- **Remittance receipts and payment confirmations**
- **Any assessments, reassessments, or correspondence**
- **Interest/penalty notices** (if any)

Keeping these organized prevents surprises and speeds up responses if CRA asks questions.

---

## Pre-year-end and year-end “ready-to-file” checklist
### 8) Year-end financial information
Before your fiscal year-end closes, gather:
- **Trial balance** or accounting system trial balance export
- **List of outstanding expenses and accruals** (known but unpaid)
- **Bank reconciliation status** at/near year-end
- **Fixed asset register** (additions, disposals, and dates)

### 9) Owner-related documents (commonly missed items)
Depending on your situation, compile:
- **Capital asset purchase/sale documents** (invoices, settlement statements)
- **Shareholder loan balance details** (opening, repayments, ending)
- **Personal contributions made through the business** (and the support for them)
- **Any compensation details** relevant to filings

### 10) Payroll year-end slips (when applicable)
If you have employees:
- **T4 information workflow** (from payroll provider)
- **Reconciliation between payroll summaries and accounting records**
- **Final payroll reports for the last payroll period**

---

## A simple system that works (choose one)
You don’t need anything fancy. Pick a method you’ll stick to:
- **Digital folder by year + month** (e.g., 2026/01… 2026/02…)
- **One “Receipts” folder** with consistent naming (vendor-date-amount)
- **Monthly reports export** from your accounting software

If you do this monthly, you’ll enter tax season with everything already organized—rather than scrambling to reconstruct the year.
